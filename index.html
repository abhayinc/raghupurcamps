Serolsar Lake: a quiet forest lake near Jalori Pass

Serolsar Lake (also written Seroalsar or Serolsar Tal) is a small, calm lake set deep in forest just below Jalori Pass. The water mirrors the sky, shifting between green, blue and grey. On the bank stands a small temple dedicated to Budhi Nagin, and locals treat the lake as sacred.

THE SACRED LEGEND

The Legend of Mata Budhi Nagin & The Pure Waters

According to ancient Himalayan folklore, Serolsar Lake is the sacred abode of Mata Budhi Nagin—the mythical mother of sixty Nag (serpent) deities and protector of the inner Seraj valley. The lake is revered with supreme piety by the villagers of Kullu and Mandi.

One of the most mystical phenomena observed by trekkers is how impeccably spotless the lake remains throughout the year. Legend says a tiny species of sacred bird, known locally as Aabi, diligently sweeps the lake. Whenever a dry oak leaf falls into the water, the bird swoops down to retrieve it, ensuring the goddess’s sanctuary remains mirror-clear.

"Shoes are traditionally kept well away from the perimeter, and no one dares pollute its sacred waters. The silence here belongs to another era."

Sacred Lake Sanctuary Etiquette

Strictly No Swimming

The lake is holy water dedicated to the serpent goddess Budhi Nagin. Stepping into the water or washing is strictly prohibited.

Remove Footwear at Shrine

Footwear must be taken off before approaching the temple precincts and the circumambulation path.

Leave No Trace

Carry all plastic, bottles, and wrappers back with you to Jalori Pass. Respect the sacred birds that keep the waters clean.

Trek to the Forgotten Citadel of Mandi Kings

Raghupur Fort (also known as Raghupur Garh) was originally constructed centuries ago by the rulers of the Mandi princely state as a strategic surveillance outpost guarding against invasions from Kullu and Suket. Today, its historic stone walls, ancient water reservoir, and majestic 360-degree ramparts overlook the Great Himalayan National Park.

HERITAGE & TRAIL

Walking the Ancient Ridge of Kings

The trail starts right from Jalori Pass, breaking away from the tourist crowd into dense oak and rhododendron woodlands. Within 20 minutes, the forest canopy gives way to dramatic alpine meadows (locally called Bugyals) that roll on endlessly towards the sky.

As you near the top, the ancient dry-stone masonry of Raghupur Fort emerges like a crown upon the ridge. Walk through the surviving ramparts, discover the historic sunken stone pond that provided freshwater during medieval sieges, and stand where sentries kept watch over two Himalayan kingdoms.

"On a clear afternoon, you can count dozens of snow peaks, and watch hawks glide on thermals far beneath your feet."

Trip Planning & Route Guide

Jalori Pass sits between the outer Seraj and inner Seraj regions, connecting Shimla and Kullu districts. Here is our direct guide on reaching us smoothly and comfortably.

From Delhi / Chandigarh

Overnight Bus via Aut Tunnel

Take any overnight Volvo/Semi-sleeper bus headed towards Manali or Kullu. De-board at the Aut Tunnel (approx. 11-12 hours from Delhi).

From Aut, take a local taxi or bus to Banjar & Jibhi (30 km), then continue up to Jalori Pass (12 km).

Self Drive / Bike

Scenic Road Route

Route: Delhi → Chandigarh → Mandi → Pandoh → Aut Tunnel → Larji → Banjar → Jibhi → Jalori Pass.

Well-paved highway until Banjar. The final 12 km from Jibhi to Jalori is a steep mountain ascent with spectacular views.

Flight / Train

Nearest Airport & Railhead

Airport: Bhuntar Airport (Kullu) is 50 km away.

Railway: Chandigarh Railway Station (260 km) or Kalka.

We can arrange private sanitized cab pick-ups directly from Bhuntar or Chandigarh on request.
